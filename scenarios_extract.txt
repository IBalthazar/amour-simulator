Scenario 1: Responding to a Vulnerable Confession
Scenario
Your best friend is going through a breakup. You have been interested in them for
years, but nothing ever happened because you were afraid to confess. While
feeling lost and uncertain, your friend confides in you and admits feelings you
have been hoping to hear for years. You are unsure whether they are in the right
state of mind to begin a relationship.
Questions to consider
This could be your one and only chance to be with your crush, but would it
feel right?
Is acting on their confession wrong, even if they consent?
Just because you can act on the opportunity, does that mean you should?
Relation to the text
Consent is not enough. Desire should not come at the expense of someone’s
vulnerability. Just because there is consent does not necessarily mean that the
interaction is moral or ethical (Perry).
Responses
A. Tell them you feel the same, but suggest waiting until they’ve had time to
process the breakup before starting anything.
B. Take the opportunity and pursue the relationship now because their
feelings may be genuine regardless of the timing.
C. Tell them how you feel, but leave the decision entirely in their hands and
trust them to know what they want.
D. Keep your feelings to yourself for now because acting while they are
vulnerable would make you uncomfortable.

Scenario 7: Loving Someone Despite Their Past
Scenario
You have been in a loving and happy relationship for two and a half years.
However, the relationship did not begin this way. Early on, your partner did
something disturbing. You forgave them, and they have changed for the better, but
the memory still lingers. You cannot forget what happened even though they are
no longer the same person.
Questions to consider
Do you go to therapy?
Do you talk openly with your partner about the lingering memory?
Do you end the relationship even though your partner has changed?
Relation to the text
Brogard connects love to history. Her example mainly involves relationships that
are later disqualified by an action committed deep into the relationship. This
scenario asks whether love can be disqualified by something that happened in the
past despite apparent redemption. There is a difference between discovering a
dark or disturbing thing about someone after entering a relationship and knowing
about something they did beforehand but choosing to begin a relationship
anyway.
